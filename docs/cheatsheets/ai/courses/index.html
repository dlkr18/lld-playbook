Courses // AI

One path from vectors and derivatives to QLoRA, vLLM and FSDP. Every concept builds on the ones before it, with the intuition first, then the formula, a worked number, code, and the trap people fall into. Interview questions with answers close each part.

A short course built around two screening questions. Each one is really asking "have you shipped LLM features like an engineer, or only played with prompts?" Learn the concepts, build one small project so your answer is real, then use the answer kit at the end.

Part 0 · The math you actually need

Not a math course. Just the six ideas every later part leans on: vectors, matrix multiply, derivatives, the chain rule, probability, and cross-entropy. If you can do the worked numbers here by hand, the rest of the page reads easily.

Part 1 · Machine learning, the classic core

Linear and logistic regression contain the entire training story in miniature: a model, a loss, a gradient, an update, and the fight against overfitting. Neural nets just stack more of the same.

8 concepts

Part 2 · Neural networks

Stack linear layers with nonlinearities and you can approximate almost any function. This part covers how such a stack is built, how its gradients are computed, and the tricks that make deep stacks trainable.

9 concepts

Part 3 · PyTorch, practically

Write a training script you'd trust in production: correct shapes, mixed precision, accumulation, checkpoint and resume, and a debugging routine for when loss misbehaves.

10 concepts

Part 4 · Language models and transformers

From "predict the next word" to a working GPT you can write from memory: tokens, embeddings, attention, the block, the cache, decoding, and the efficiency tricks every modern LLM uses.

15 concepts

Part 5 · How an LLM is made

The pipeline from raw web text to a helpful assistant, and how to tell whether a model is actually good.

8 concepts

Part 6 · Fine-tuning: full, LoRA, QLoRA

The job post's key line: "you have fine-tuned a model yourself." Understand the math, run it on a free GPU, and report before/after numbers.

9 concepts

Part 7 · Embeddings, vector search, and RAG

Most AI products ground a model in private data. Know how text becomes searchable vectors, how nearest-neighbor search scales, and how a retrieval pipeline is built and measured.

Part 8 · Compression and fast inference

Distillation, quantization, vLLM, ONNX and TensorRT, all explained from one idea: whether a step is limited by arithmetic or by moving bytes.

Part 9 · GPUs and distributed training

How a GPU actually spends time, and how training splits across many of them. Your distributed-systems background carries most of this.

Part 10 · Projects that prove it

Reading gets you through interviews' first round; artifacts get offers. Each project below is small, and together they cover every AI line in the job post.
